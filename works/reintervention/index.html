A responsive digital body places care and damage within the same participatory system. Developed from Intervention within The Funeral, the work was presented at LUCKY777 alongside a making video and Record.

From Intervention to RE:INTERVENTION

RE:INTERVENTION develops Intervention, a work within Meta Rose: The Funeral, into a separate presentation. It was shown at the LUCKY777 group exhibition presented by Platform Presents at Abbotsford Convent on 10 and 11 October 2026. This presentation centres on the interactive RE:INTERVENTION work. It also screened a behind-the-scenes video and Record, presented as an independent moving-image artwork.

The work begins with the selves we reject while trying to sustain an acceptable image. Drawing on vanitas, the rose carries decay within its beauty, while the skeleton retains the time of a body that lived. These are two faces of the same body. Rather than remove an unwanted side and declare a positive self victorious, the work keeps care and harm, injury and return visible together. The visitor can choose a different relationship with what remains.

Participation and the responsive body

Physical buttons let visitors alter the balance and vitality of a responsive digital body. A forehead-mounted webcam and body tracking connect the person acting with the image being altered. A rectangle formed with thumb and index finger reveals a camera mask; an open five-finger gesture reveals another mask carrying the visitor’s chosen colour and optional rose name. The participant becomes both an agent within the system and a witness to the consequences of their choices.

Holding any two buttons on the Rose Human Interface for five seconds requests a capture. Care and harm do not resolve into a correct sequence: an attempt to preserve one balance may disturb another, while life may return after a destructive action. The capture preserves a chosen moment within that relationship. It records an action in the work rather than diagnosing the visitor’s feelings.

The optional Phone Hub returns the chosen colour, optional name and requested captures through a random Rose number, without requiring a legal name. Live camera processing is distinct from storing an image after the visitor requests it. Watching, declining a capture, pausing or leaving remain valid choices. The linked Phone Hub Archive is a read-only record of the historical interface and examples, with no installation connection, new capture or visitor-response collection.

Record and the making process

Record is a separate, silent looping artwork about the labour of making the installation. Hands, cable joins, shaped forms, failed tests and repeated connections remain visible. The film keeps the machine’s material body and the time required to make it in view, rather than allowing them to disappear behind an apparently seamless response.

It has no required first or last scene and makes no personal capture. Visitors may enter or leave at any point, and the film does not interpret their actions or the results of RE:INTERVENTION. Its status as an independent work is distinct from the behind-the-scenes video also screened in the LUCKY777 presentation.
