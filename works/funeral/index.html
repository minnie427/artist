A funeral for the self I have killed. What becomes of the parts of ourselves we repeatedly try to remove?

Overview

Meta Rose: The Funeral stages a funeral for the parts of the self repeatedly suppressed in pursuit of an acceptable image. It considers not only private self-rejection, but also the demands of perfection, productivity and a presentable public self. The person who kills, the person who dies and the person who remains may be the same person. The funeral creates a place to meet those rejected selves again, rather than declare them gone.

The work draws on vanitas, in which flowers and skulls hold life and decay within the same image. Here, those symbols become bodies that respond to an audience: a skeleton offers its hand, and roses release sound and light through touch. The reminder of mortality turns towards the ways we treat ourselves while alive. Beauty and injury, care and destruction can occupy the same body without cancelling one another.

Naming, Intervention, Witness and Record form a connected funeral ritual. A name made through the first encounter accompanies the figure that is acted upon and witnessed in the following works. Record brings the hands and material labour behind the installation into view. This progression does not promise treatment or a correct path to recovery; visitors retain the choice to observe, pause, leave or return.

01 / Naming

Visitors hold the skeleton’s left hand and touch living roses with the other hand to complete a circuit. Fifteen roses each carry a different response in sound, light and pixels. Sustained contact deepens a response, while the order and combination of flowers create layers within a changing audio-visual composition. The living hand, skeleton and flower become simultaneous conditions of the same circuit.

When a desired balance appears, holding both of the skeleton’s hands requests a capture. Naming comes afterwards, through a description of a side of the self the visitor has rejected and another face that existed alongside it. The second description does not correct or beautify the first. The name holds their contradiction together, translating a bodily encounter with sound and image into the visitor’s own language.

02 / Intervention

The figure named in Naming returns as a responsive body that is both rose and skeleton. Physical controls and camera-based gesture tracking allow visitors to alter its balance and vitality. Water, sunlight, poison and a monster do not form a simple set of good and bad choices: care can become excessive, and destruction need not produce a final ending. What matters is the pattern of repeated actions and the decision to change it. Death is followed by return, with traces of earlier damage still present.

A rectangle formed with thumb and index finger reveals the camera image over the figure being cared for and harmed. An open five-finger gesture reveals a mask carrying the visitor’s chosen rose colour and name. The person acting and the person watching that action meet within one image. In the Seoul configuration, holding any two buttons on the Rose Human Controller for two seconds requests a capture of the selected moment.

03 / Witness

Visitors move a hand vertically near a rose to control the speed of a moving image. Across three slow acts of witnessing, they look for an initially distorted rose-skeleton figure within images and languages drawn from funeral narratives around the world. When they feel they have found the figure, they press the rose button. This is a decision made from a particular position, rather than confirmation of a correct answer.

The distortion draws on the anamorphic presence of death in vanitas painting, where a form becomes legible from another position. Here, hand and body position change the conditions of seeing. Time is slowed rather than possessed or permanently stopped, allowing the visitor to grant themselves time to look. The clearer image does not remove death; it lets apparently opposing faces become visible within the same narrative.

04 / Record

Record is an independent, silent looping film about the material and bodily work of making the exhibition. Hands cut and join cables, shape forms, test sensors and restart failed connections. The film leaves these joins, errors and repetitions visible instead of presenting the completed machine as seamless magic. It is a work in its own right, rather than a promotional explanation of the other installations.

There is no required first or last scene. A visitor may enter or leave at any point while the hands on screen continue their work. Record does not interpret the outcomes of the interactive works or classify the audience. It preserves the labour and making time that would otherwise disappear behind the finished surface.

Participation, traces & material time

The colour chosen on arrival, the name formed in Naming and visitor-requested images are connected through a random Rose number. The name and traces travel between the works, making one choice available to encounter again in another form. The Phone Hub returns these records without turning them into a score, personality type or objective reading of emotion. Naming and recording remain choices, and participation without a record is possible.

The living roses in Naming change through touch, drying and decay. The artificial roses and specimen-like materials of Intervention and Witness hold a different relationship with time, retaining a form against change. The work does not rank them as real and fake: one disappears because it is alive, while another remains because it does not change in the same way. Photographs retain particular material states without reversing their loss.

The linked Phone Hub Archive preserves the historical interface and example captures. It does not connect to the installation, make new captures or collect visitor responses.

Presentation

Presented as a solo exhibition at Mijin Floor, Yeonnam-dong, Seoul, from 15 to 18 August 2026, as part of Seoul Fringe Festival.

Technical details

Seoul Fringe Festival / 2026

Medium
Interactive audio-visual installation in four parts: Naming, Intervention, Witness and Record.
Materials
Living roses, artificial rose specimens, skeletal model, conductive contacts, sound, light and moving image.
Dimensions
Variable according to venue and installation configuration.
Tools / software
TouchDesigner (primary software); touch-responsive system, camera-based gesture tracking, responsive controller, connected phone interface and looping film.
Artist / production
Minnie Park, concept, exhibition planning and production.
