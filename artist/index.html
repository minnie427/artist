Short Bio

Interactive audio-visual installation / Research / Moving image

Minnie Park is a Korean-Australian interactive audio-visual artist working between Seoul and Melbourne. Her installations combine living roses, tactile interaction, sound, light and moving image to examine affect as embodied, relational and culturally situated. Through Meta Rose—begun in 2024 and including The Meta Kibun Project—she develops distinct participatory systems around emotional ambivalence, culturally shaped feeling, relational touch and memory.

Her 2025 Honours research at RMIT University examined audience accounts through The Meta Kibun Project, using interviews and surveys alongside an interactive exhibition. Park is also a co-founder of TouchCollective, an artist-led community centred on TouchDesigner, and develops workshops connecting creative practice, embodied participation and technical experimentation.

Artist Statement

From The Pinkbird to Meta Rose

I painted The Pinkbird in 2021 without realising that the bird had no legs. Looking at it later, I saw myself: unable to fly freely, yet unable to find anywhere to settle. I had been painting in oils at home through Melbourne’s lockdowns, repeatedly reaching for pink. It felt like me, and reminded me that I had not let go of art. The colour made me feel alive. During a deeply painful period in my life, I found myself struggling with feelings I could neither explain nor reconcile. I wanted to understand how emotions could contradict one another and yet feel equally real. Meta Rose began from that need. I found in the rose an embodiment of the ambivalence I was trying to understand: its beauty, scent and softness coexist with thorns, inseparable within the same living form. To me, it can hold sadness and joy at once, without ceasing to be beautiful. This coexistence became the visual and material language of the work. Over time, my relationship with it has changed. I am learning to attend to difficult feelings without allowing them to obscure everything else, to recognise the thorns while embracing the flower more fully. Interactivity is essential to my audio-visual practice because I believe participation can deepen affective experience, bringing people into a more personal encounter with the work and opening questions about their own feelings. Through this practice, I pursue a lifelong ambition to express human emotion through computational language, data and equations, working towards a form through which subjective experiences might become more understandable and shareable. Meta Rose is where I continue searching for my own equation for human emotion. I hope these encounters offer others some of the comfort and strength that pink and art have given me, and the space to find a pink of their own.
